Language vs. Dialect: What's the Difference?

The words “language” and “dialect” are useful, but the boundary between them is not determined by grammar alone.

A structural view

Linguists can compare pronunciation, vocabulary and grammar to identify differences among varieties. Some varieties are highly similar; others may differ substantially. Mutual intelligibility can be useful evidence, but it is not a perfect universal test.

The social side

People also use language labels as markers of identity. Education, institutions, literature, history and political boundaries can influence whether a variety is called a language or a dialect.

Why the question gets complicated

Two varieties can be structurally close while having separate language identities. Conversely, varieties called “dialects” can differ enough that communication is difficult. There is no single rule that settles every case.

A useful takeaway

Instead of treating “dialect” as a synonym for “incorrect language”, it is better to think of dialects as varieties of a language or speech community with their own patterns. Linguistic description focuses on how people actually use those varieties.
