What Is Morphology?

Morphology is the branch of linguistics concerned with the internal structure of words and the meaningful pieces that make them up.

Start with the word itself

Consider a word such as “unhelpful.” It can be analysed as smaller meaningful parts: “un-”, “help”, and “-ful”. These pieces are called morphemes. A morpheme is a unit that contributes meaning or grammatical information.

Free and bound morphemes

Some morphemes can stand alone. In “helpful”, the base “help” can appear as an independent word, so it is a free morpheme. Prefixes and suffixes such as “un-” and “-ful” normally need to attach to another form, so they are bound morphemes.

Derivation and inflection

Derivational morphology can create a new word or change its grammatical category, as in “teach” → “teacher”. Inflectional morphology modifies a word to express grammatical information while generally keeping its basic lexical identity, as in “walk” → “walked”.

Why morphology matters

Morphology helps explain why words behave the way they do. Once you learn to spot bases, affixes and grammatical endings, unfamiliar words become easier to analyse rather than simply memorise.
