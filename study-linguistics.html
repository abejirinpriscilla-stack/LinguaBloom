How to Study Linguistics Without Memorising Everything

Linguistics becomes easier when you build a network of concepts instead of trying to memorise a pile of definitions.

1. Learn the big map

Before drilling details, identify the major areas: phonetics studies speech sounds, phonology studies sound systems and patterns, morphology studies word structure, syntax studies sentence structure, and semantics studies meaning.

2. Turn definitions into examples

After learning a definition, create or find two examples. Then explain why each example fits. If you cannot explain the connection, the definition probably has not become usable knowledge yet.

3. Compare related concepts

Make contrasts such as derivational vs. inflectional morphology or phonetics vs. phonology. Comparison forces your brain to notice the boundary between concepts.

4. Practise retrieval

Close your notes and explain a concept from memory. Then check what you missed. This is more useful than repeatedly rereading the same paragraph.

5. Build mini exam answers

For written exams, practise a compact structure: definition, main points, examples, distinction or application, then a brief conclusion. This turns knowledge into an answer you can actually deploy.
