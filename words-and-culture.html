When Words Carry Culture

Vocabulary does more than label objects. Words can preserve relationships, histories, social expectations and distinctions that matter to the communities using them.

Language stores distinctions

Every language divides up experience in its own ways. A community may have especially detailed vocabulary around activities, relationships, landscapes or social practices that are important in everyday life.

Names can tell stories

Personal and place names can carry history, hopes, family connections and cultural references. Understanding the context behind a name can reveal information that a direct translation may miss.

Expressions need context

Idioms and culturally specific expressions often resist word-for-word translation. Their meaning can depend on shared knowledge, social conventions and the situation in which they are used.

Keep the culture attached

Studying a language becomes richer when vocabulary is connected to the people who use it. A good explanation should therefore give context instead of treating words as isolated entries in a dictionary.
